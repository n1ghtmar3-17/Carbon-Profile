Almost 71% of the Earth is covered by the ocean; it contains more than 97% of the Earth’s water and greatly aids in absorbing warmth from the climate (NOAA). The ocean is essential to life on Earth, as it nurtures marine life, economies of people, and so much more. However, due to negative climate change, the ocean climate is worsening.

Ocean Warming

Ocean warming is the gradual increase of temperature of the ocean. It is a main cause of many other effects below, but also key to more hurricanes and other tropical storms and their increase in intensity (NASA).

Melting Sea Ice

As the climate warms, the ocean absorbs more heat and becomes warmer. The warmer air and warmer sea causes sea ice, like glaciers at the poles, to melt faster. Without sea ice covering the darker-colored ocean, less heat is reflected, as sea ice reflects more heat than the open ocean. This means the ocean absorbs more and more heat, which is detrimental to the climate and the marine environment (NASA).

Marine Heatwaves

Marine heatwaves are short periods of unusually warm waters. While marine heatwaves can be natural, climate change has caused these heatwaves to increase in frequency and intensity, because of the climate warming the waters. Because of these heatwaves, the ecosystem often fluctuates with it, with some species moving to different areas and others, unable to move, being mortally harmed. One example of an unmovable species is coral.

Coral builds coral reefs, which are essential to the environment due to its symbiotic nature with many marine animals. In addition, “over half a billion people depend on reefs for food, income, and protection” (NOAA). However, due to the warmer oceans, the symbiotic relationship between the algae in coral and the coral can become stressed, causing the algae to leave the coral and the coral losing its color and becoming “bleached”. Coral bleaching is a sign that the coral is stressed and more likely to die (NOAA). This doesn’t just happen to a few coral - “84% of the world’s coral reefs impacted in the most intense global coral bleaching event ever [From 1 January 2023 to 30 March 2025]” (ICRI).

Ocean Acidification

Ocean acidification is when carbon dioxide from the air dissolves in the oceans to form carbonic acid, which lowers the pH (increases acidity) of the ocean. In fact, since the pre-industrial era, the amount of hydrogen ions in seawater has risen, making the ocean roughly 25% more acidic than it was in the 1700s (EPA). Making oceans more acidic harms marine life, especially life like coral, oysters, and other shellfish that rely on calcium carbonate to build their exoskeletons and shells because carbonate ions become less abundant from the series of chemical reactions with carbon dioxide. Ecosystems surrounding these species are disrupted due to their declining health (NOAA; EPA).

Effects on Us

The effects of climate change on the ocean directly affects humans economically and mortally. “As many as 3.6 billion people live in areas of high vulnerability to climate change impacts such as droughts, floods, heat waves, extreme weather events and sea-level rise” (conservation.org). The impacts of climate change on marine life also indirectly affects humans because the ecosystem of the Earth is interconnected.

“Land plays an important role in the climate system. Agriculture, forestry and other types of land use account for 23% of human greenhouse gas emissions. At the same time, natural land processes absorb carbon dioxide equivalent to almost a third of carbon dioxide emissions from fossil fuels and industry.” -Jim Skea, Co-Chair of IPCC Working Group III

Natural Disasters

Due to climate change, the rate and intensity of natural disasters are increasing. Hurricanes and tropical storms rise faster and more frequently from being fed by warming ocean waters. Warming temperatures dry out vegetation, leading to droughts, while the shifting rainfall patterns increase the duration of these droughts. Due to drier conditions and warming temperatures, it has “doubled the number of large fires between 1984 and 2015 in the western United States” (C2ES).

Land Productivity

Climate change can negatively affect agriculture and the condition of the land. Although hotter temperatures can mean longer growing seasons in some regions, which is good for food production, these temperatures can also stress plants, cause more evaporation, and attract more pests and bugs in other regions, which can cause more erosion, drain the soil of nutrients, and harm agriculture plants (EPA).

Furthermore, due to warmer temperatures, shifts in wind and other factors change rain precipitation destinations and amounts, which could cause more flooding and rainfall in some areas. Other areas would experience more droughts and desertification, where land becomes too dry and damaged to have vegetation (NASA, NOAA).

Biodiversity

Due to the shifting weather and temperatures, many species are forced to migrate, which disrupts the natural ecosystem they once inhabited and now inhabit. Some species may not adapt fast enough and are thus threatened (United Nations). According to the World Meteorological Organization, “climate change is one of the contributing factors…nature is declining globally at rates unprecedented in human history, and that about one million species are threatened with extinction” (WMO).

Permafrost

Rising temperatures due to climate change are starting to thaw permafrost, which holds almost half of the amount of organic carbon in Earth’s soil. As permafrost thaws, carbon dioxide and methane are released, which can severely intensify climate change and global warming. It covers 24% of land in the northern hemisphere, which means when it thaws, the terrain will be irreversibly changed for the people living on permafrost (Bykova et al.).

Effects on Us

Humans live on land, so the effects of climate change on land affect humans greatly. The increasing amount of natural disasters can cause mortal and economic harm, changes in agriculture can cause food insecurity, and desertification and permafrost degradation can cause forced migration.

The air we breathe is the main character of climate change - the word “climate” says enough about the importance of air. However, climate change causes the air we breathe to be a distributor of harmful particles.

Ozone Pollution

Ground-level ozone, a major component of smog, forms through chemical reactions between sunlight and greenhouse gases or air pollutants which are sped up by higher temperatures of the climate. This increased ozone pollution makes breathing harder and worsens health conditions like asthma (Shipman, EPA).

Wildfire Smoke

Hotter temperatures and drier land lead to larger and more frequent wildfires. These fires release harmful gases and particulates (fine particles) into the air which spreads far beyond the fire zones (WMO).

Effects on Us

Humans breathe in air to live, but with polluted air from the ozone and wildfire smoke, it is more likely for people to get respiratory and cardiovascular problems.
